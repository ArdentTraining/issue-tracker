How to read these numbersVisits, not students. Active time only. Medians throughout.
The grain is a lesson visit, not a student: one student opening a lesson three times counts as three visits. Active time is counted only while the tab was visible and focused (30 seconds per heartbeat, derived server-side), so it is time spent on the lesson rather than time the tab sat open. Medians throughout, and visits shorter than one heartbeat are reported separately as bounces rather than dragged into the median.
Time per lesson…

Median active time per engaged visit. The bar shows the median; the range is the middle half. Bounces are excluded from the median and shown separately, so a quick look-and-leave never drags a lesson's time down.

Engaged versus bounced…

A bounce is a visit that ended before the first 30-second heartbeat: opened, glanced at, closed. A high bounce rate on a teaching lesson is worth a look; on a quiz it usually just means someone checking what is in it.

Where visits stop…

Share of visits reaching each slide. A cliff between two bars is where the lesson loses people. Quiz packages have no slides, so they do not appear here.

The first slide…

Across every instrumented lesson, the step from the first slide to the second loses far more visits than every later step put together. Once a visit reaches slide two it nearly always runs to the end, so this one step is where the attention goes. Counted on engaged visits only, so an eleven-second glance is not held against the lesson.

Reported problems against traffic…

Issue reports from the tracker set against visits, so a lesson that is genuinely faulty separates from one that is simply busy. A lesson with reports and no telemetry is listed too: that is a lesson we are being told about and cannot see.

Long and interrupted…

Lessons where visits run long and people keep switching away. Neither on its own says much: a long lesson should take a while, and one tab switch is nothing. Together they suggest people are going off to look something up, which is worth a read of the lesson. A soft signal, not a verdict.

When people study…

Lesson visits by hour and day, UK time, from the activity ledger. Each hour counts the visits that sent anything in it, so a student on a lesson for two hours counts in both. Bank holidays and school holidays are marked; weather is the daily mean of five UK regions (London, Manchester, Bristol, Glasgow, Belfast) from Open-Meteo, and the school-holiday bands are a typical council calendar rather than every council's. Correlations need a year of data to mean much; the ledger has been keeping it since telemetry started.

How the tracker is used…

Anonymous counts from the bug tracker's own interface over the last 30 days: which pages are opened, which controls are pressed, and how issue panes end. By role (instructor, developer, course team, admin), never by person. A dead end is a pane opened, nothing pressed in it, and shut within ten seconds. The point is to find controls nobody uses and paths that take too many steps, and trim from evidence.

Questions…

Per-question results from the quizzes and module assessments, broken down to the individual answer box rather than the whole question. Marks earned is the better number than fully correct, because a six-box question marked wrong may have had five boxes right. Read the attempts column beside any percentage: the question bank randomises, so one question may have been seen by a dozen people and another by sixty, and their rates are not comparable. Only lessons exported as xAPI appear here.

Per-student reporting is parked, by decision

Everything on this page is real. This view is the one thing the data cannot answer, and it is showing you exactly why rather than dressing visit counts up as student counts.

What reviving it would take
A deliberate second reason Even a browser-level identifier (a random id stored on the device, no name attached) is personal data under UK GDPR, so it would need a privacy-policy change, a lawful basis and a retention period. That was considered on 12 August 2026 and parked on purpose. See PRIVACY.md. What is on this page needs none of that: no names, no accounts, no persistent identifiers, no IP addresses.
Signing in changed who can read this, not what is collected This page now sits behind your tracker account. That governs who may look at the aggregates. It adds no identity to the store and does not make any of this data personal: the lessons still report anonymously and cannot be tied to a student, including by us.
